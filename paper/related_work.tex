\paragraph{2D and 3D Cross-Instance Correspondence Datasets.}
A wide range of datasets have been proposed for cross-instance correspondence learning, primarily in 2D. Early benchmarks such as PF-PASCAL~\cite{ham2018pascal_proposalflow} and SPair-71k~\cite{min2019spair71k} provide sparse keypoint annotations across object instances under significant viewpoint and scale variation, while CUB~\cite{WahCUB_200_2011} provides fine-grained bird keypoints. AP-10K~\cite{yu2021AP10k}, although originally introduced for animal pose estimation, has been used by GeoAware-SC~\cite{zhang2024tellingLeftFromRight} to derive 2D semantic correspondence supervision from shared pose keypoints. Similarly, MARCO~\cite{cuttano2026marco} derives semantic correspondences from large-scale pose estimation data~\cite{xu2022poseforeverything}, and Jamais Vu~\cite{mariotti2025jamaisvu} extends SPair-71k with SPair-U to evaluate generalization to unseen keypoints. Despite their success, these datasets remain image-based, focus largely on humans and animals, and rely on human-annotated keypoints or pose supervision, providing only sparse annotations.

In 3D, existing correspondence datasets, including SHREC~\cite{pratikakis2016shrec_16,dyke2019shrec_19}, SMPL-based human datasets~\cite{zuffi2017menagerie}, and deformation benchmarks~\cite{groueix2018b3dcodeddataset}, primarily focus on geometry and often target a single category or articulated shapes such as humans or animals. They also typically lack texture or appearance information, limiting their ability to support projected 2D--2D correspondences for training image-based correspondence models. More recently, DenseMatcher~\cite{zhu2025densematcher} introduced DenseCorr3D, a dataset of colored meshes with part-level annotations across 23 categories, but it does not provide scalable fine-grained point-to-point supervision. KeypointNet~\cite{you2020} provides the largest 3D cross-instance keypoint dataset for rigid objects, but it is limited to 16 categories with sparse annotations, averaging roughly 12 keypoints per object.

Overall, existing datasets rely on expensive and sparse human annotations, limiting scalability. In contrast, our work automatically generates large-scale approximate semantic-geometric correspondences directly between 3D object instances, enabling scalable supervision across diverse rigid object categories and naturally supporting 3D--3D, 3D--2D, and 2D--2D correspondence tasks.


\paragraph{2D Semantic-Geometric Correspondences.}
Semantic correspondence aims to identify analogous locations across different instances of the same category.
However, existing work differs in how it treats the geometric specificity of the correspondence.
A first line of work studies semantic-only correspondence, often formulated as cross-instance image alignment~\cite{gupta2023asic,ofriamar2023neuralCongealing,Barel:ECCV:2024:SpaceJAM,Hirsch:NeurIPS:2025:FastJAM}.
In this setting, geometrically symmetric points may be treated as equivalent if they share the same semantic identity; for example, points on the left and right wings of an airplane may both be considered valid under flipped alignments.
Accordingly, these methods often allow image flips or flipped keypoint annotations during evaluation on datasets such as SPair-71k and CUB~\cite{min2019spair71k,WahCUB_200_2011}.

A second line of work follows the standard semantic correspondence protocol~\cite{hedlin2023unsupervisedstablediffusion,luo2023DiffusionHyperFeaturesTimeSpace,cho2022cats++,mariotti2024sphericalmap,zhang2023taleTwoFeatures,zhang2024tellingLeftFromRight,cuttano2026marco,wandel2025semalign3d,mariotti2025jamaisvu}, typically evaluated on SPair-71k~\cite{min2019spair71k} without annotation flips.
This setting requires models to resolve both semantic identity and geometric orientation when such distinctions are well defined, such as front/back, left/right, or top/bottom.
However, for symmetric categories such as tables, bottles, or potted plants, geometric distinctions can be inherently ambiguous, and evaluation often depends on image-relative location rather than a unique semantic-geometric correspondence.

A third perspective, emphasized by GeoAware-SC~\cite{zhang2024tellingLeftFromRight}, isolates cases of geometry-aware correspondence in which appearance similarity alone is insufficient and resolving the correct match requires reasoning about object orientation or relative part location. Their results show that existing methods often underperform on such cases, motivating our goal of generating richer semantic-geometric supervision at scale. 

\paragraph{3D Semantic Keypoints and Correspondence Methods.}
Recent 3D correspondence methods increasingly combine appearance and geometry to obtain semantic matches across shapes. DenseMatcher~\cite{zhu2025densematcher} learns dense 3D semantic descriptors for textured meshes by projecting multi-view 2D foundation-model features onto mesh vertices, refining them with a 3D network, and computing correspondences with functional maps; its main focus is category-level manipulation from a limited set of annotated objects. UKPGAN~\cite{you2022ukpgan} is an unsupervised 3D keypoint detector that predicts sparse salient keypoints for individual shapes, but does not directly establish cross-instance correspondences. Skeleton Merger~\cite{shi2021skeletonmerger} learns aligned sparse keypoints through skeleton-based reconstruction, yielding category-level keypoint alignment across instances. However, these methods remain limited to sparse landmarks and do not provide scalable dense cross-instance correspondence supervision for broad rigid-object collections.
