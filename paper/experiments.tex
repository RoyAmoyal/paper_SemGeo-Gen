\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate the proposed correspondence-generation pipeline in two complementary settings. First, we assess whether the generated correspondences can improve real-image semantic correspondence models when used as synthetic pretraining data (\autoref{spair71k_eval}). Second, we directly evaluate the quality of the generated 3D correspondences against sparse human-annotated 3D ground truth(\autoref{annotation_quality}).
All experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU.

\subsection{Experimental Data}
\label{sec:exp_data}

Our method assumes collections of rigid 3D objects that are approximately rotation-aligned, providing a weak category-level geometric prior. In this work, we use two complementary 3D sources, 
\textbf{KeypointNet}~\cite{you2020} and \textbf{Objaverse-OA}~\cite{orient_anything}, 
to validate the proposed pipeline. 
%
% 
\textbf{KeypointNet} provides sparse human-annotated semantic keypoints on approximately aligned 3D object models. It has relatively few rigid categories, but many objects per category, making it suitable for evaluating how well the generated correspondences  agree with human semantic annotations. These annotations are used only for evaluation, not for training or correspondence generation.
%
%
\textbf{Objaverse-OA} provides far broader category and shape diversity, containing over 100 rigid categories after curation, but without correspondence annotations. \autoref{sup:qualitative}  shows example correspondences generated by our method on several Objaverse-OA categories beyond those covered by existing correspondence datasets.
%


For downstream SPair-71k~\cite{min2019spair71k} experiments,
we generate synthetic correspondences from nine rigid categories
(aeroplane; bicycle; boat; bus; car; chair; motorbike; train; tvmonitor), excluding non-rigid and strongly symmetric objects.
We evaluate two settings: fine-tuning on the same Rigid-9 categories,
and fine-tuning on all 18 categories, additionally including
Non-rigid-7 (bird; cat; cow; dog; horse; person; sheep)
and Symmetric-2 (bottle; potted plant).
Both settings use the same Rigid-9 synthetic pretraining data.

\subsection{Results on SPair-71k}
\label{spair71k_eval}
We evaluate the synthetic 2D correspondence supervision generated by our pipeline on SPair-71k~\cite{min2019spair71k}, a standard benchmark for cross-instance semantic correspondence.
Concretely, we generate 12K synthetic 2D training pairs per category from diverse 3D object instances sampled from Objaverse-OA~\cite{orient_anything}. To preserve category and shape diversity in the synthetic pretraining data, we avoid pair-level semantic filtering in this experiment. We focus on 9 rigid categories with well-defined 3D semantic-geometric structure: aeroplane; bicycle; boat; bus; car; chair; motorbike; train; tvmonitor. We report reproduced results for recent methods, including GeoAware-SC~\cite{zhang2024tellingLeftFromRight}, Jamais Vu~\cite{mariotti2025jamaisvu}, and MARCO~\cite{cuttano2026marco}, alongside reported results for earlier methods in \autoref{tab:spair_pck010}.
%
For the state-of-the-art GeoAware-SC and MARCO, we also evaluate the effect of our generated data in three training regimes: training on SPair-71k only; joint training with our projected correspondences; pretraining on our generated correspondences followed by fine-tuning on SPair-71k. 

Since our data is generated from synthetic 3D objects, a synthetic-to-real domain gap remains,
and naive joint training gives mixed results. In contrast, the standard pretrain-and-finetune
regime consistently improves both evaluated models. GeoAware-SC~\cite{zhang2024tellingLeftFromRight}
improves from 79.36 to 83.73 mean PCK (+4.37), and MARCO~\cite{cuttano2026marco}
improves from 86.34 to 89.30 (+2.96). These gains are obtained without any architectural
modification, highlighting that even approximate semantic-geometric correspondences provide
a useful geometric-semantic training signal that is easier to obtain than manual annotations.
Additional qualitative examples in \autoref{fig:qual_marco_main} further illustrate the
improved semantic-geometric consistency obtained after pretraining on our generated data.

\noindent\textbf{Beyond rigid categories.}
We also investigate whether synthetic pretraining on rigid
categories transfers to non-rigid and symmetric objects.
As \autoref{tab:spair_all18} shows, pretraining GeoAware-SC
on our Rigid-9 synthetic correspondences followed by
fine-tuning on all 18 SPair-71k categories improves
PCK@0.10 by 3.72 on rigid categories, 1.29 on non-rigid
categories, and 2.39 overall, compared to real-only training.
Notably, these gains extend to categories excluded
from synthetic pretraining.
\subimport{../tables}{spair71k_18cat}

We additionally compare against DenseMatcher~\cite{zhu2025densematcher}
under the same training protocol, which performs similarly to
real-only training (81.83 vs.\ 82.09 PCK overall).
DenseMatcher combines semantic features with geometric refinement
but requires costly part annotations, with only 5 animals, 5 chairs,
and 6 vehicles in its released training data.
Its functional-map matching may also struggle with large structural
variations, consistent with the symmetry-mismatched and structurally
inconsistent correspondences we observed on diverse Objaverse-OA instances.
In contrast, SemGeo-Gen generates dense approximate semantic-geometric
correspondences from scalable collections of rotation-aligned 3D models,
without part or correspondence annotations.



\subsection{Evaluation of Annotation Quality}
\label{annotation_quality}

We evaluate our generated correspondences on KeypointNet~\cite{you2020},
which provides human-annotated semantic keypoints and correspondences
on approximately rotation-aligned 3D objects.
We assess both \textbf{3D--3D} correspondence accuracy against human
annotations and \textbf{3D--2D} accuracy after projection into image space.

\noindent\textbf{3D--3D evaluation.}
For each category, we evaluate all model pairs within a subset,
retaining pairs where at least $60\%$ of each model's points have
a DINOv2 match with cosine similarity above $0.575$
($68\%$ of pairs retained on average).
Within retained pairs, we prune correspondences with endpoint
feature similarity below $0.575$.
For each ground-truth query keypoint, we select the correspondence
with the nearest source point, provided its distance is below
$0.05$ times the target bounding-box diagonal.
Keypoints without a surviving correspondence are excluded from PCK,
and their fraction is reflected in the GT-used rate.
Predictions are correct if their target locations lie within
$\alpha$ times the target bounding-box diagonal of the GT keypoint.
We average results over three random subsets of 100 models per
category (38 and 90 models for Cap and Helmet, respectively),
as shown in \autoref{tab:keypointnet_table}.
Our method achieves 93\% PCK@0.10, 97\% PCK@0.15,
and 99\% PCK@0.20, with a median GT-used rate of 57.6\%.
Reduced coverage reflects semantic pruning that avoids
uncertain matches between structurally different instances.
Detailed per-label results and evaluable keypoint counts
appear in \autoref{Sup:per_label}.
On the same pairs, we also outperform Back to 3D~\cite{wimmer2024back}
in both 1-shot and 3-shot settings without requiring keypoint
annotations (see \autoref{sup:backto3d}).


\subimport{./}{keypointnet_exps}

\noindent\textbf{3D--2D evaluation.}
We further evaluate our generated 3D correspondences in image
space by projecting them onto randomly rendered views.
As in our data generation, only correspondences whose target
points are visible in the rendered image are retained.
Across 16 categories, we achieve 94.2\% PCK@0.10 in 2D,
with an average GT-used rate of 45.3\% of ground-truth keypoints in the rendered views.
On the same visible correspondences, 3D PCK@0.10 reaches 95.1\%,
compared to 93\% in the original 3D--3D evaluation,
which includes all found keypoints regardless of visibility.
The slight decrease may result from geometric inaccuracies
in the approximate 3D correspondences, which can introduce
projection errors or visibility inconsistencies in image space.
Nevertheless, the results demonstrate that correspondence
accuracy is largely preserved when transferred to 2D.
The full evaluation protocol and per-category results
are provided in \autoref{sup:3d2d_eval}.

\subsection{Ablations and Hyperparameter Sensitivity}
\label{sec:ablation}
\begingroup
\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
We summarize the main findings of our ablation studies here and defer detailed quantitative analyses, per-category tables, and additional sensitivity experiments to \autoref{sup:ablation_study} and \autoref{Sup:per_label}.
\\

\noindent\textbf{Hyperparameters.}
Performance is stable across a reasonable range of the CPA smoothness weight \(\lambda_{\mathrm{smooth}}\) and semantic threshold \(\tau_{\mathrm{sem}}\). As detailed in \autoref{sup:ablation_semantic_filtering}, \(\tau_{\mathrm{sem}}\) controls an accuracy--coverage trade-off, and \(\tau_{\mathrm{sem}}=0.575\) provides a practical balance between retaining reliable correspondences and preserving sufficient coverage. For the CPA smoothness weight, we observe only minor variation in KeypointNet \cite{you2020} PCK across tested values, likely because the sparse annotations are mostly concentrated on salient corners and extremities. Qualitatively, however, \(\lambda_{\mathrm{smooth}}=0.3\) better preserves the source object structure while still allowing sufficient local deformation to align with the target, producing more coherent dense correspondences in regions not captured by the sparse KeypointNet annotations. We therefore use \(\lambda_{\mathrm{smooth}}=0.3\) and \(\tau_{\mathrm{sem}}=0.575\) in all experiments.

\noindent\textbf{Registration model.}
We compare continuous piecewise-affine (CPA) registration with a global affine transformation and thin-plate splines (TPS); see \autoref{sup:ablation_registration} for details. Affine performs substantially worse at all PCK thresholds, while TPS achieves accuracy closer to CPA. CPA nonetheless achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy at most evaluated thresholds and is approximately \(3\times\) faster in our setup, likely because its tetrahedral piecewise-affine parameterization is local, whereas TPS relies on a global radial-basis deformation.



\par\noindent\textbf{Semantic filtering.}
Semantic filtering consistently improves correspondence quality (see \autoref{sup:ablation_semantic_filtering}). Without correspondence-level or pair-level filtering, geometric registration alone achieves over \(84\%\) PCK@0.10. Adding correspondence-level semantic pruning improves performance to about \(91\%\) PCK@0.10, while pair-level filtering yields a further improvement to about \(93\%\) PCK@0.10 on the retained pairs. We additionally provide a detailed per-semantic-label analysis in \autoref{Sup:per_label}, following the evaluation protocol of~\cite{wimmer2024back}, as well as a qualitative analysis of DINOv2-based semantic filtering and correspondence pruning in \autoref{sup:supp_semantic_analy}.


\noindent\textbf{Sensitivity to rotation misalignment.}
We evaluate robustness to imperfect canonical alignment.
Mean PCK@0.10 decreases from 93\% to 89\% and 85\%
under 5$^\circ$ and 10$^\circ$ rotation perturbations,
respectively, but drops substantially for larger rotations,
confirming the importance of approximate alignment
(see \autoref{sup:rotation_sensitivity}).
